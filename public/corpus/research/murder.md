# Death & investigation
The engine is a change in what can be known. A death, missing person or disputed cause can create a question; the script must preserve the difference between a question and an accusation.

## Let evidence change the story
Introduce a relevant observation, identify who recorded it, then explain what it does and does not establish. A toxicology finding may establish a substance; it does not automatically establish administration, intention or perpetrator. Medical language needs a plain explanation supported by the source.

## A usable sequence
Human stakes → discovery → initial account → evidence that changes the account → competing interpretations → supported outcome or bounded uncertainty.

## Repair a weak reveal
**Unsupported:** “The poison proved her husband had planned everything.”
**Responsible:** “The laboratory reported poison in the sample. At trial, prosecutors alleged her husband had supplied it. The defense challenged the link between that sample and him.”

A factual script does not need a solved puzzle. It needs an accurate account of why the question remains open.

## End where the evidence ends
Name the person, the last verified case status, and the precise missing link. Do not name an uncharged person as the killer, solicit amateur accusations, or promote speculation as a new theory.
