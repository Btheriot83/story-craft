# Relationship & betrayal
Begin with who matters to whom. A lover, spouse, friend or relative can be the emotional center without making the story a romance.

## Find the relationship in the record
Look for supported choices: a marriage, a separation, a documented affair, a letter, an argument described in testimony. Ask what each changes. Do not infer private passion from a photograph or assume jealousy because two people were partners.

## A usable sequence
Establish the bond → introduce the documented rupture → follow a consequential choice → show how the record connects it to the crime or inquiry → return to a named person’s consequence. Leave out any step the evidence cannot support.

## Repair a weak sentence
**Unsupported:** “Consumed by jealousy, he decided she would never leave.”
**Responsible:** “She had filed for separation. Prosecutors later argued that the filing supplied a motive; his defense disputed that account.”

The second version creates pressure while preserving attribution. It does not prove the alleged motive.

## Do not force a happy ending
RWA defines genre romance around a central love story and an optimistic ending. Relationship-led crime has a different contract: an honest human consequence. A tragic relationship is not a failed romance novel.
