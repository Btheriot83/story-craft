# Trial & disputed truth
A verdict, appeal or later reassessment can change the meaning of everything the listener has just heard. The drama comes from competing claims and real consequences, not from treating a courtroom as a machine that reveals perfect truth.

## Keep the voices separate
“The prosecution argued…” identifies an allegation. “A witness testified…” identifies testimony. “The jury convicted…” identifies a verdict. “The appeal court overturned…” identifies a later legal outcome. None is interchangeable with an omniscient narrator’s conclusion.

## A usable sequence
A person facing a consequential allegation → the state’s central claim → the material defense or contradiction → what the decision actually resolved → the subsequent status → the principal’s specific consequence.

## Crime of passion is not an explanation
The legacy URL is retained for compatibility. Jealousy, coercion, betrayal and premeditation require evidence and attribution. Do not romanticize violence as inevitable love or assume a legal defense applies across places and eras.

## Compress without distorting
Keep a hearing only if it changes liberty, credibility, evidence, or the audience’s understanding. Translate the ruling accurately. A conviction overturned for an unfair trial is not automatically an exoneration; a charge dismissed is not a conviction.

For a wrongful-conviction question, state exactly what later evidence or ruling changed and what remains unresolved. Check the latest status before writing.
