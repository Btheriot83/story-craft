# A verdict is not the end of the record
**Fictional exercise.** This drill uses the [full fictional case packet](/examples/love); no real case is described.

## Before
“Justice finally prevailed. Daniel was innocent, and love had survived the nightmare.”

This invents innocence, imposes a romance ending, and abandons Ada and Ruth.

## After
“The appeal court overturned Daniel’s conviction because of the trial’s handling of identification evidence. Prosecutors did not try him again. Ruth still had Ada’s letter, but no established answer to who had poisoned her sister.”

The revision states the ruling, subsequent action and human uncertainty. In a real case, each requires a source, locator and current-status check.

## Your turn
Write two endings of 40–60 words: one emphasizing the legal consequence for Daniel, one emphasizing the unresolved question for Ruth. Neither may imply that reversal identified the killer.

## Grade the alternatives
Which ending resolves the opening’s actual promise? Which principal has driven the story? Does the final line add a specific consequence, or merely repeat that the case is mysterious?

An unresolved case can have a strong ending. What fails is an abstract shrug—or an invented solution.
