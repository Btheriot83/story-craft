# Evidence is not a twist
**Fictional exercise.** Use the [full fictional case packet and narration](/examples/love) before this drill.

## The bad transformation
“The laboratory found poison. At last, Ruth knew Daniel was the killer.”

The first sentence corresponds to fictional F3. The second invents Ruth’s knowledge and leaps from a reported substance to perpetrator identity.

## The responsible transformation
“The laboratory reported poison in the sample. It did not identify who had administered it. Prosecutors would have to connect that finding to Daniel.”

This version creates the next question without pretending to answer it. In a factual case, even the statement about the prosecution’s task needs legal and procedural context.

## Your turn
Write 80–100 words carrying the laboratory finding into the trial. Include the prosecution’s theory, the defense’s challenge, and one plain-language explanation of why sample handling matters. Do not add a new witness, a confession, an affair or a symptom.

## Answer criteria
A successful paragraph distinguishes substance, custody, administration and identity. It preserves uncertainty and advances the causal chain. It does not bury the later reversal to make a conviction sound final.

Read the paragraph aloud; underline every claim and attach its packet ID. If an ID does not support the whole sentence, split or narrow the sentence.
