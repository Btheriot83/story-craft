# Sources — Story Craft Bibliography

Prefer primary craft sources. Summaries only; no substantial copyrighted prose. Accessed/verified Sep 2026 unless noted.

## Classic craft books & theory

1. **Edgar Allan Poe — Review of Hawthorne’s *Twice-Told Tales* (1842) / “single effect”**  
   - URL: https://www.eapoe.org/works/criticsm/gm442hn1.htm  
   - Takeaway: A short tale should aim at one predominant impression; every sentence should help produce that effect. Design the ending first, then select incidents toward it.

2. **Edgar Allan Poe — “The Philosophy of Composition” (1846)**  
   - URL: https://www.poetryfoundation.org/articles/69390/the-philosophy-of-composition  
   - Takeaway: Backward design — know the dénouement, then construct toward unity of effect and length suited to one sitting (maps to ≤5-min Story Craft pieces).

3. **Blake Snyder — *Save the Cat!* (beat sheet); micro-short application**  
   - Micro-short essay: https://savethecat.com/beat-sheets/save-the-kitten-stc-and-micro-shorts (J. Elizabeth Martin, 2017)  
   - Takeaway: Full STC beats can compress into ~4 minutes if you jump in cold, sacrifice B-story/subplots, and land a finale that recontextualizes the opening image.

4. **John Yorke — *Into the Woods*; five-act structure**  
   - URL: https://www.johnyorkestory.com/five-act-structure/  
   - Takeaway: Five girders (inciting → escalating attempts → midpoint revelation → crisis → resolution) prevent sagging middles even when acts are seconds long. Midpoint must change meaning or stakes.

5. **Robert McKee — *Story* (desire/conflict principles)**  
   - Secondary overview: https://literarysofa.com/2014/02/06/a-novel-view-of-robert-mckees-story-substance-structure-style-and-the-principles-of-screenwriting/  
   - Takeaway: Conscious want + unconscious need; conflict is opposition to desire. At micro length, one clear desire and one opposing force are enough.

6. **François Truffaut / Alfred Hitchcock — suspense vs. surprise (“bomb under the table”)**  
   - Summaries: https://nofilmschool.com/alfred-hitchcock-and-francois-truffaut-explain-surprise-vs-suspense (Hellerman, 2023); https://www.davidbordwell.net/blog/2013/11/29/hitchcock-lessing-and-the-bomb-under-the-table/  
   - Takeaway: Inform the audience for sustained suspense; reserve pure surprise when the twist *is* the highlight. Both modes are valid for ≤5-min crime/passion pieces — choose deliberately.

## Short-form / flash craft (recent)

7. **NYC Midnight — “How to Write Flash Fiction”**  
   - URL: https://www.nycmidnight.com/howtowriteflashfiction  
   - Takeaway: One narrative thread, ≤3–4 characters, get-in-late/get-out-early dialogue, cut subplots and backstory; every scene must serve arc, growth, and forward motion.

8. **River / Chandler Supple — short-story structure & endings (2025–2026)**  
   - URL: https://rivereditor.com/guides/how-to-structure-short-stories-2026  
   - Takeaway: Multi-function details; start later / end earlier; single-effect focus; satisfying endings deliver opening promise, show change (or meaningful failure), resolve central tension, feel inevitable in retrospect.

9. **Hannah Lee Kidder — “How To End a Short Story” (2023)**  
   - URL: https://hannahleekidder.com/2023/07/05/how-to-end-a-short-story-strategies-examples/  
   - Takeaway: Connect ending to beginning (echoed image/line with new meaning); experiment with close types; last paragraph sticks — make it intentional and earned.

10. **Writers.com — “Better Endings: Surprising Yet Inevitable”**  
    - URL: https://writers.com/feature/better-endings-surprising-yet-inevitable-and-astonishing  
    - Takeaway: Causal chain + emotional logic must lead to the ending; meaning comes from change; map “because” links from middle to close.

11. **Writers in the Storm — “Reward Your Readers with a Battle that Earns the Ending” (2023)**  
    - URL: https://writersinthestormblog.com/2023/10/reward-your-readers-with-a-battle-that-earns-the-ending/  
    - Takeaway: Crisis → climax → short resolution; write with the end in mind; protagonist must earn the right to the final confrontation; brief resonant coda, not a laundry list.

12. **NYC Midnight — “Plot Twists in Short Stories”**  
    - URL: https://www.nycmidnight.com/blog/plot-twists  
    - Takeaway: Twist must be foreshadowed, character-consistent, and load-bearing; simplify so the twist has space; motive/yearning make surprise feel inevitable.

## Genre-specific

13. **Jami Gold — beat sheets for short stories (2018)**  
    - URL: https://jamigold.com/2018/07/how-can-we-use-beat-sheets-with-short-stories/  
    - Takeaway: Shorts need fewer beats (≈4 major turns: ~25% into conflict, ~50% stake/goal shift, ~75% hope loss, 80–95% climax). Stay within ~3–5% of marks; drop B-story/subplots.

14. **Romance short-read / instalove beats (Ash Brousseau, Medium)**  
    - URL: https://medium.com/@ash.brousseau94/romance-beats-for-instalove-how-to-write-short-reads-1e8a3884acfe  
    - Takeaway: Compress to five moves: meet-cute → right-but-not-ready → demonstrate attraction → tear apart via *internal* conflict → earned reunion + HEA/HFN. Misunderstanding-only breakups fail.

15. **Gwen Hayes / commercial romance ending contract (HEA vs HFN summaries)**  
    - e.g. https://www.writingmastery.com/blog/hea-vs-hfn-understanding-happy-endings-in-romance-fiction ; https://plotprose.com/the-dark-moment-romance/  
    - Takeaway: Dark moment must grow from wounds; reunion requires on-page change/grand gesture specific to the wound; ending answers the opening question and keeps genre promise (HEA or HFN — not ambiguous fade).

16. **B.K. Stevens — mystery openings / conflict hints (“The Last Blue Glass” craft notes)**  
    - URL: http://www.bkstevensmysteries.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/04/First2Pages-LastBlueGlass.pdf  
    - Takeaway: Quiet open that plants discord, then hard pivot (death / revenge intent); aftermath-then-flashback timeline suits short crime; reader challenge = who/why before the act completes.

17. **Fair-play mystery ending craft (2020s craft blogs synthesizing Christie tradition)**  
    - e.g. https://usenoren.ai/blog/how-to-write-a-fair-play-mystery ; https://www.writingmanager.com/sylvia/agatha-christies-mystery-formula/  
    - Takeaway: All decisive clues before reveal; reveal reclassifies old evidence (not new facts); solution should feel simpler in hindsight; surprise on first read, inevitability on second.

18. **CrimeReads — “Revenge in Fiction” roundtable (Gaylin, Audrain, et al., 2022)**  
    - URL: https://crimereads.com/revenge-in-fiction-author-discussion/  
    - Takeaway: Revenge/passion plots need obsession + cost; aftermath matters (loss remains); strongest stories are character-driven so thirst for revenge is visceral; easily justified revenge is often least interesting.

## Hooks, audio, short video

19. **Ann Handley — “Mastering the Art of the Cold Open”**  
    - URL: https://annhandley.com/cold-open-2/  
    - Takeaway: Airdrop into a specific moment that plants questions; warm opens report and lose attention; cold opens ground action and force the reader to stay for bearings.

20. **Ira Glass / This American Life — anecdote + reflection storytelling**  
    - URL: https://www.thisamericanlife.org/extras/ira-glass-on-storytelling ; https://current.org/2016/02/tips-from-ira-glass-on-better-radio/  
    - Takeaway: Sequence of actions that raise a question, then a beat of meaning; repeat until the “bigger something” is earned — useful for spoken ≤5-min Story Craft delivery.

21. **Short-form video narrative structures (TikTok/Reels/Shorts, 2026)**  
    - URL: https://shortzly.com/blog/short-form-video-storytelling-structures  
    - Takeaway: Hook early; middle needs connective structure (open loop, micro-payoffs, problem→escalation→payoff); completion depends on middle + end, not hook alone. Maps to spoken cold open → escalate → close the loop hard.

22. **Charlie Euchner — working backwards for surprise (Vertigo / prior puncturing)**  
    - URL: https://thestoryarchitect.substack.com/p/work-backwards-to-create-surprise  
    - Takeaway: Build a complete-feeling false prior, then puncture it; don’t merely hide facts — make the wrong story feel whole until it can’t.

## Gaps / notes

- Primary McKee/Yorke/Snyder book text not pasted (copyright); principles summarized from public secondary pages and author sites.
- “Crime of passion” as Story Craft genre blends literary jealousy/rage arcs (e.g. *Kreutzer Sonata* tradition) with thriller/revenge craft — not a legal brief. Distinguish from fair-play *murder* (puzzle) by emotion-first irreversible act + aftermath.
- Spoken timing assumes ~130–150 wpm; 600–750 words ≈ 4–5:30; target trim to ≤5:00.
