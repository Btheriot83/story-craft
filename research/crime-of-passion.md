# Crime of Passion — Craft at ≤5 Minutes (~600–750 words)

**Genre ID:** `genre-crime-of-passion`  
**Promise:** An emotionally overwhelming, relationship-rooted irreversible act (violence, ruinous revenge, or equivalent moral rupture) driven by jealousy, betrayal, humiliation, or grief-rage — plus the **heat and cost** of that act.  
**Not the same as Murder lane:** Murder = fair-play *puzzle*. Crime of passion = *emotional inevitability* and aftermath. Clues optional; **psychology and point-of-no-return** mandatory.

**Cast budget:** 2–3 (lover / rival / witness). The triangle or dyad *is* the plot.

---

## Genre distinction (agents: do not conflate)

| | Murder | Crime of Passion |
|--|--------|------------------|
| Engine | Concealment + fair clues | Obsession + rupture |
| Reader question | Who/how? | Will they / what does it cost / can they live with it? |
| Ending | Reveal reclassifies evidence | Act + moral/emotional consequence |
| Tone | Cool craft, puzzle pleasure | Hot, claustrophobic, tragic or pyrrhic |
| Time | May reconstruct past crime | Often continuous pressure → eruption |

Literary ancestors (paraphrase only): jealous rupture narratives (*Kreutzer*-type); revenge fiction where grief/obsession/rage fuse (CrimeReads 2022). Story Craft treats “passion” as narrative heat, not a courtroom excuse.

---

## Beginning (0:00–~1:00)

### Goals
1. **Cold open in the wound:** Infidelity evidence, humiliation in public, the rival’s laugh, the empty side of the bed — not a Wikipedia bio of the marriage.
2. **Emotional stake fast:** What was loved / what was stolen / what pride cannot bear.
3. **Pressure cooker:** Hint the act is *possible* (Hitchcock: show the “bomb” of rage; or Stevens: quiet dinner that will become poison).
4. **Sympathy without endorsement:** Make desire/pain understandable; do not glamorize harm. Adult craft — consequence is part of the art.

### Hook patterns
| Pattern | Then… |
|---------|-------|
| Finding the message / lipstick / receipt | Memory of better love collapses into plan or spiral |
| Public slight at a party | Private humiliation → stalking attention |
| Reunion that should heal | Old betrayal reopens mid-embrace |
| “Special drink” / loaded gun in drawer | Ordinary hospitality over a ticking moral clock |

---

## Middle (~1:00–~3:30)

### Goals
- **Narrow options** (thriller craft): each beat removes a dignified exit (talk, leave, forgive).
- **Escalate obsession** (Gaylin et al.): grief + rage + fixation; objectivity dies.
- **Show the relationship’s real texture** — passion crimes fail when the bond was never felt on-page.
- Midpoint: confirmation (they *did* betray / they *will* leave) or a chance at mercy the protagonist refuses.

### Escalation ladder
1. Evidence accumulates (or jealousy invents — clarify which for the audience).
2. Confrontation aborted or partial.
3. Rival or lover appears vulnerable — temptation of mercy.
4. Clock / location traps them together (kitchen, car, cabin).
5. Point of no return approaches; body language precedes decision.

### Anti-sag
- No legal procedural detours.
- No new subplot (inheritance, gang).
- Keep POV tight — passion is experiential; distance kills heat.
- If using dramatic irony (we know the suspicion is false), that *is* the bomb under the table — milk anticipation, then decide whether the act still happens (tragic error) or is stopped (rare; must still cost).

### Character depth
One **identity threat** (“without you I am nothing” / “I am not a fool”). Show competence elsewhere so the collapse is specific. Power imbalance often intrinsic (CrimeReads) — name it in behavior.

---

## Ending (~3:30–5:00 | REQUIRED — the act is not enough alone)

The irreversible act may land at climax, but the **ending** is act + **aftermath meaning**. Stopping on the gunshot with no emotional or moral beat = weak. Stopping on “then she walked away” with no cost = fail.

### Ending must deliver
1. **Point of no return on-page** (choice or explosion after pressure).
2. **Immediate consequence** — body, silence, sirens, self-recognition, pyrrhic “win.”
3. **Emotional truth** — revenge/passion rarely heals the loss (CrimeReads: loss remains; explore aftermath).
4. **Resonant image** — echo the opening token (blue glass, message thread, wedding ring) now ruined or weaponized (Kidder; Stevens token logic).
5. **Moral clarity without lecture** — reader feels the weight; no PSA paragraph.

### Strong closing patterns (passion)
| Pattern | Punch |
|---------|-------|
| **Pyrrhic completion** | They “win” and instantly feel the hollow |
| **Wrong target / tragic error** | Irony: bomb was false; act remains |
| **Mercy refused then regretted** | One second too late |
| **Survivor’s stillness** | After violence, domestic ordinary continues grotesquely |
| **Confession to the dead** | Love speech over the consequence |
| **Justice after passion** | Arrest as relief or further humiliation |

### Ending anti-patterns (auto-fail)
- Act happens off-page; we only hear about it.
- Clean getaway, no inner cost, smirk ending as “cool.”
- Soft fade before the choice.
- Sudden genre flip to cozy puzzle without passion heat.
- Glorified violence without human consequence.

### Ending QA
- [ ] Is the irreversible moment audible/visible on the page?
- [ ] Does the last beat change how we read the love that started it?
- [ ] Would cutting the final 30–40 words remove the moral/emotional payload? (Must be yes.)
- [ ] Is this clearly *not* a fair-play whodunit ending?

---

## Timeline patterns
- Continuous night of eruption (best for heat).
- Years-later dinner party revenge with embedded marriage flashback (Stevens).
- In medias res aftermath → reconstruct the passion → present reckoning.

## Reveal mechanics
Optional identity reveal; primary “reveal” is often **self** — who the passionate actor has become. If there’s a twist (wrong suspect of betrayal), plant the misread early; ending punctures the prior (Euchner).
