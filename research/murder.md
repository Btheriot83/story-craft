# Murder — Craft at ≤5 Minutes (~600–750 words)

**Genre ID:** `genre-murder`  
**Promise:** A death (or imminent murder) + investigation/understanding; **fair-play reveal** that recontextualizes planted clues.  
**Cast budget:** Victim + killer + ≤2 others (detective/witness). Puzzle clarity > ensemble.

**Sibling note:** Unlike *crime of passion*, murder here emphasizes **concealment, method, and fair clues** — cool craft over heat. Passion may exist as motive, but the engine is *who/how/why as puzzle*.

---

## What works at this length

Flash murder = **one crime, one false theory, one true solution**. Use dual timelines lightly: chronological crime vs. revelation order (reader discovery). Hitchcock choice: either **suspense** (audience knows danger) or **twist-as-highlight** (surprise ending) — don’t muddle both.

Poe: design the final effect (dread, shock, moral chill) first; every sentence serves it.

---

## Beginning (0:00–~1:00)

### Goals
1. **Cold open:** Body, almost-body, or ominous ordinary that *will* become evidence (Stevens: quiet then shatter; Handley: airdrop).
2. **Stake in 30–60s:** Who cares (detective’s debt, spouse’s freedom, killer’s cover about to fail) + clock if any.
3. **Contract:** Signal fair-play or pure suspense mode. Don’t promise puzzle then solve with off-page confession only.

### Hook patterns
| Pattern | Progress by… |
|---------|----------------|
| Aftermath kitchen / “special drink” intent | Flashback to first warning signs |
| Discovery of body mid-sentence | Immediate wrong suspect + one odd detail |
| Bomb-under-table (Hitchcock suspense) | Ordinary chat while audience knows the threat |
| Killer’s calm routine | Crack in the alibi planted as “normal” |

### Avoid
- Five suspects with equal bios (no room).
- CSI jargon dump before human stake.
- Opening autopsy lecture with no POV desire.

---

## Middle (~1:00–~3:30)

### Goals
- **Escalate** investigation or concealment pressure.
- Plant **≥2 fair clues** with dual readings (surface vs. true) before reveal (fair-play craft 2020s / Christie tradition).
- **One strong red herring** that feels complete (Euchner: false prior).
- Midpoint: theory that “solves” it — then puncture.

### Escalation moves
1. New evidence that deepens the wrong theory.
2. Witness lie or partial truth.
3. Killer forced to improvise (clock / almost-caught).
4. Protagonist’s personal stake worsens (accused, next victim, career).

### Anti-sag
- No suspect parade. Deepen *two* competing explanations.
- Every beat answers one question and opens another (thriller open-loop stack).
- Timeline: keep crime-clock vs. story-clock clear; contradictions break trust.

### Character depth
Detective/witness = one bias (guilt, love, pride) that *misreads* a clue. Killer (if POV) = competent calm + one tell. Depth = motive that fits character yearning (NYC Midnight twists).

---

## Ending (~3:30–5:00 | REQUIRED punch)

Fade-out, unsolved shrug, or “police will handle it” without on-page answer = **FAIL** for this catalog’s murder lane (unless explicitly tagged experimental — default = solved or decisively twisted).

### Ending must deliver
1. **Reveal as proof, not announcement** — reassemble clues already shown (Christie-grade; fair-play blogs).
2. **Recontextualization** — at least one earlier detail flips meaning (Sixth Sense / Vertigo lesson via Euchner & Hitchcock exception for twist-as-highlight).
3. **Inevitable in hindsight** — surprising first listen, obvious on replay (Writers.com; Christie formula summaries).
4. **Consequence beat** — arrest, escape with cost, moral aftertaste — not just “it was X.”
5. **Resonant close** — echo opening image (last glass, noose letter, wet ring) with new valence (Kidder; River end-earlier but *hard*).

### Strong closing patterns (murder)
| Pattern | Punch |
|---------|-------|
| **Clue parade in 3 lines** | Detective names three planted details → one conclusion |
| **Killer’s tell exposed** | Habit from open proves guilt |
| **False prior punctured** | Complete wrong story collapses |
| **Audience-knew / character-learns** | Suspense release (Hitchcock) |
| **Moral chill coda** | Justice incomplete; truth costs the living |

### Ending anti-patterns (auto-fail)
- Twin / secret passage / poison introduced only at end.
- Confession without prior fair clues.
- Twist unrelated to character motive.
- Soft “maybe we’ll never know.”
- Long explanation after emotional peak (cut past the ending — Peace/Makkai craft notes).

### Ending QA
- [ ] List facts the solution needs — all appear before reveal?
- [ ] Does reveal make the story *simpler*?
- [ ] Last 20 seconds = irreversible status change?
- [ ] Opening image pays off?

---

## Timeline patterns
- Linear discovery.
- Aftermath → flashback of marriage/crime → present completion of revenge/arrest (Stevens model).
- In medias res kill → reconstruct.

## Reveal mechanics
Plant → misdirect → midpoint false solve → crisis (hero wrong/endangered) → reveal reclassification → short consequence. Prefer **interpretation twist** over **new information**.
