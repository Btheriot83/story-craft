# Cross-Cutting Craft — All Story Craft Genres

Applies to love, murder, and crime of passion at **≤5:00 / ~600–750 words**.

---

## Universal shape: Beginning → Middle → Ending

Per Poe, Yorke, Jami Gold, River (2026), and short-video retention craft: shorts still need **setup, escalation, and payoff**. Flash is not a vignette without change. Acts may last 45–90 seconds, but all three phases are mandatory.

| Phase | Time (guide) | Job |
|-------|----------------|-----|
| **Beginning** | 0:00–1:00 | Cold open, care hook, stake, genre promise |
| **Middle** | 1:00–3:30 | Escalation, pressure, midpoint turn, no sag |
| **Ending** | 3:30–5:00 | Crisis → climax → resonant close (**hard punch**) |

**Agent rule:** Scripts missing a strong ending fail QA even if the hook is brilliant.

---

## Beginning — cold opens that *progress*

Handley: airdrop into a specific moment that plants questions; warm opens report and lose listeners.

### Cold open checklist
- [ ] First sentence has sensory or conflict specificity.
- [ ] A question forms in the listener’s mind within ~10 seconds.
- [ ] By ~60 seconds we know who wants what and what it costs.
- [ ] Opening plants a detail that the **ending will reuse**.

### Progression rule
A cold open is not a gimmick if beat 2 **answers a little and asks more**. Open loops (Shortzly) must close by the end.

Ira Glass (spoken): anecdote (actions raising a question) + reflection (why it matters). In fiction, “reflection” may be dramatized realization rather than essay.

---

## Middle — anti-sag at micro length

Yorke: midpoint revelation or irreversible shift. Jami Gold: ~50% goal/stake change; ~75% hope crash.

### Micro middle tools
1. **Attempt → complication → worse attempt** (causal “because” chain — Writers.com endings essay).
2. **Open loops stacked** — answer one, raise one (thriller craft).
3. **Micro-payoffs every ~20–30s** of spoken time (Shortzly) — new info, choice, or status shift.
4. **Narrow choices** — remove exits (thriller outline craft).
5. **One setting or one night** when possible — location pressure substitutes for length.

### Middle fails when…
- Characters discuss feelings without new decisions.
- Travel/summary montages replace scenes.
- New cast members arrive after 2:00.
- Midpoint is “more of the same.”

---

## Ending — first-class, non-negotiable

Sources converge: **surprising yet inevitable**; seed in prior text; crisis → climax → short resolution; echo beginning; earn the right to the final beat; cut past the soft landing.

### Universal ending requirements (all genres)
1. **Decisive change** in relationship, knowledge, or moral status.
2. **Earned by prior causality and emotional logic.**
3. **Closes the open loop** from the cold open.
4. **Resonant last image/line** (not a shrug).
5. **No fade-out** — if silence, it must be *charged* silence after a clear act.

### Ending strength scale (agents)
| Score | Meaning | QA |
|-------|---------|-----|
| 3 | Punch + resonance + genre promise kept | PASS |
| 2 | Clear climax but soft coda | REVISE |
| 1 | Twist/act without meaning | FAIL |
| 0 | Fade, summary, or unresolved shrug | FAIL |

### Genre ending flavors
- **Love:** HEA/HFN via costly choice + specific gesture; echo open.
- **Murder:** Fair reveal reclassifies clues + consequence; echo open.
- **Passion:** Irreversible act + aftermath cost; echo token; no glorified shrug.

## Timeline presentation

| Pattern | Best for | Risk |
|---------|----------|------|
| Linear continuous | Passion heat; love single-night | Must escalate constantly |
| In medias res → fill | Murder; passion aftermath | Confusing clocks |
| Aftermath → flashback → present | Murder revenge; passion reckoning | Flashback stuffing |
| Open on end-state, rewind | All (if end-state is vivid) | Spoil without suspense plan |

**Clarity rule:** Listener always knows *when* we are. Tag time shifts in one clean phrase.

---

## Reveal mechanics

| Mode | Use when | Hitchock note |
|------|----------|---------------|
| Suspense (audience knows) | Passion bomb; murder countdown | Prefer for duration of dread |
| Mystery (audience learns with POV) | Fair-play murder | Clues before reveal |
| Twist highlight | Identity / motive flip | Exception: surprise *is* the highlight — still foreshadow |
| Emotional reveal | Love truth; passion self-knowledge | Plant fear early |

Euchner: make a **false prior** feel complete, then puncture it. NYC Midnight: twist must be character-consistent and load-bearing.

## Character depth at ≤750 words

NYC Midnight: few named characters; reveal via action/reaction; skip physical-inventory intros.

### Depth recipe
1. One desire (McKee want).
2. One need/wound (may be unconscious).
3. One contradictory trait (competence + blindness).
4. One choice that costs.
5. Change or tragic refusal to change by the end (Writers.com meaning-from-change).

---

## Spoken / audio performance notes

- Target **130–150 wpm**; 650 words ≈ 4:20–5:00.
- Prefer short paragraphs and audible turnbeats for performers.
- Glass structure helps narration-heavy pieces; pure dramatic scenes can omit overt “reflection” if the final image *is* the meaning.
- Retention: hook alone insufficient — middle structure + ending payoff drive completion (Shortzly).

---

## Compression checklist (all genres)

- [ ] ≤3–4 named characters.
- [ ] One thread; no B-plot.
- [ ] Multi-function details (River).
- [ ] Dialogue late-in/early-out (NYC Midnight).
- [ ] Start later / end earlier — but **end on punch**, not on convenience.
- [ ] Single effect (Poe) named before drafting.
- [ ] Ending planned first (Poe Philosophy of Composition; Writers in the Storm).
